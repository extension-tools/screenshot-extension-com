How to annotate a screenshot in Chrome

Add arrows, boxes, text and step numbers to any web page or image, hide private details, then copy, download or print the result.

A plain screenshot shows what is on the screen. An annotated one shows what matters: the button to fix, the field to fill in, the step to take next. In this guide you will capture a page in Chrome, mark it up with arrows, boxes, text and step numbers, hide private details, and save the result as PNG, JPEG or PDF.

You can do all of it in the extension, or start right here on this page with an image you already have.

Quick answer

  1. Add Screenshot Extension to Chrome and pin it to the toolbar.
  2. Open the page and choose Full Page, Selected Area or Visible Area. The capture opens in the editor.
  3. Pick a tool and draw: Rectangle, Arrow, Text, Step numbers, Blur or Draw.
  4. Click Download for PDF, PNG or JPEG, or Copy to paste the image anywhere.

Annotate a page in the extension

The extension captures the page and opens it in its own editor, so you never switch to another app. Three steps take about a minute.

Step 1. Capture the page

  1. Click the Screenshot Extension icon in the Chrome toolbar.The popup opens with three capture options.
  2. Choose Full Page for the whole page, Selected Area to drag a frame, or Visible Area for what is on the screen.Full Page shows its progress while it scrolls; the other two capture at once.
  3. Wait for the editor tab to open.Your capture is in the middle, with the editing tools above it.

Step 2. Mark what matters

  1. Click Rectangle and drag around the part you want people to look at.An orange box appears. Orange is the default color.
  2. Click Arrow and drag from empty space toward that part.The arrow points from where you started to where you let go.
  3. Click Text, click on the image and type a short note.The note stays where you clicked; you can move it later with Select.

Step 3. Hide private details

Before you share, cover names, emails and numbers. Choose Blur and drag over the area. Blur is drawn into the image when you download it, so the original text can’t be recovered from the file.

Tip: pick the color before you draw Choose the color and thickness first, and every new arrow and box will use them. See how to change them below.

Where annotated screenshots help most

  • Bug reports. A box around the broken element and one line of text save a long description.
  • Design feedback. Arrows and notes on the live page show exactly which spacing or color to change.
  • How-to guides. Step numbers turn one screenshot into a short instruction.
  • Sharing data safely. Blur hides personal details before the image leaves your computer.

Change color, size, position and thickness

Every arrow, box, line and note can be changed after you draw it. Click Select, click the annotation, then use the controls in the second row of the toolbar.

SettingHow to change itGood to know
ColorClick the color circle and pick one of six colorsOrange, red, green, blue, violet, navy. Orange is the default
ThicknessMove the stroke slider or type a value next to itThicker lines read better on large, full-page captures
SizeDrag a corner handle of the selected annotationBoxes, arrows and blur areas resize the same way
PositionDrag the selected annotation with SelectUse Undo if it lands in the wrong place
TransparencyNot available as a settingTo soften a part of the image, use Blur; to frame it, use Rectangle

Change the color of an arrow you already drew

  1. Click Select in the second toolbar row, then click the arrow.Handles appear at both ends of the arrow.
  2. Click the color circle and pick a new color.The arrow changes at once; new annotations use this color too.
  3. Move the stroke slider to make the line thicker or thinner.The number next to the slider shows the exact value.

Text notes, shapes and step numbers

Beyond arrows and boxes, three tools help you explain more on one image.

Text notes as comments

Use Text to leave a comment right next to the thing it describes. Keep notes short: one action per note, like “Move this up” or “Use the brand blue”.

Shapes

Rectangle frames an area, Arrow / Line points or underlines, and Draw lets you circle something by hand. Mix them: a box to frame, an arrow to point, a note to explain.

Step numbers

Click Step numbers, then click each place in order. The circles count up on their own (1, 2, 3), so the reader follows the steps without reading a word.

When the image is ready, choose how it leaves the editor. The Download button remembers the format you picked last time.

  1. Click the arrow next to Download and pick PDF, PNG or JPEG.The button now shows the format you chose.
  2. Click Download.The file goes to your Downloads folder with a readable name based on the page.
Save before you close the editor

The editor doesn’t autosave annotations. If you close it with unsaved edits, it asks “Discard unsaved edits?”. Choose Keep editing, then download or copy the image.

Print a screenshot with annotations

Annotations print exactly as you see them, because they are part of the image.

  1. In the editor, choose PDF in the Download menu and click Download.You get a PDF with the image and every annotation on it.
  2. Open the PDF from your Downloads folder in Chrome.It opens in Chrome’s PDF viewer.
  3. Press Ctrl + P (⌘ + P on a Mac) and choose your printer.For a long page, pick “Fit to page” or a larger paper size so the text stays readable.

Scanned PDFs and text you can’t select

A scanned PDF is a picture of a page. Its text looks like text but can’t be selected, searched or edited. A screenshot works the same way: it is an image too.

  • To mark up a scan, open the PDF in Chrome, capture the page with Visible Area and annotate it like any other screenshot.
  • To fix wrong text, cover it with Blur or a Rectangle and type the correct text on top with Text.
  • To keep text readable, zoom the PDF in before you capture. The capture keeps the sharpness you see on screen.
  • If capture is blocked, take a screenshot with your system tool and open it here with Add image.

FAQ

Can I edit an annotation after I draw it?

Yes. Click Select, then click the annotation to move it, resize it, or change its color and thickness. Undo and Redo work for every change.

Can I annotate an image that isn’t a web page?

Yes. Use Add image in the popup or the editor, or drop the file on this page. PNG and JPEG files are supported.

Is Blur safe for passwords and personal data?

Blur is drawn into the downloaded image. Cover the whole area, including the edges of the text, and check the file before you send it.

Which format should I choose?

PNG for interfaces and text, JPEG for photos and smaller files, PDF for printing and sending documents.

Where do I find screenshots I made earlier?

Click History in the popup or in the editor toolbar. It lists your earlier captures.
